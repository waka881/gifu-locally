The plastic and wax food in Japanese restaurant windows is called *sampuru* (from "sample"), and much of it comes from Gujo Hachiman, a castle town in central Gifu. The Gujo Tourism Federation says the town has the largest food-sample production in Japan, and that the craft began more than 80 years ago when Takizo Iwasaki was inspired by melted candle wax ([Gujo Tourism Federation](https://en.tabitabigujo.com/appeal/taiken-experience/foodsamples/)). This guide covers where to try it, using the Federation, Gifu Prefecture's tourism site and the workshops' own pages.

## Which workshop should you choose?

- **Sample Village Iwasaki** if you arrive by train: it is about 5 minutes from Gujo Hachiman Station, and it is run by Iwasaki Co., Ltd. The tempura-and-lettuce set is its featured project ([Gujo Tourism Federation](https://en.tabitabigujo.com/appeal/taiken-experience/foodsamples/)). The site lists a second Iwasaki branch, Ryukyo no Sato, that closes for winter from December 21 to February 20 ([Iwasaki](https://www.iwasakimokei.com/)), so make sure you book the main store in winter.
- **Sample Kobo** if you want a museum too: the experience fee includes entry to its retro art museum, which is ¥500 for adults on its own ([Sample Kobo](https://samplekobo.com/)). Its site posted a notice on October 28, 2026 about a closure affecting the experience, so check the notice or call before you go.
- **Yama-no-naka no Sanpuru-ya** if you are driving north toward Shirotori and the Gujo ski resorts.

The Federation says the sweets tart is simple enough for small children, and that children under 3 at Iwasaki should be with an adult (same source).

## How do you book a food sample workshop?

Iwasaki says experiences need advance booking ([Iwasaki](https://www.iwasakimokei.com/)), and both booking systems are in Japanese. If you don't read Japanese, the simplest route is to ask your hotel to call, or phone yourself with the date, time and number of people. Pick a morning or early-afternoon slot: Iwasaki's last experience is at 15:00.

## FAQ

### Why is Gujo Hachiman famous for food samples?

The Gujo Tourism Federation says Gujo Hachiman has the largest food-sample production in Japan, and that the craft started more than 80 years ago with Takizo Iwasaki ([Gujo Tourism Federation](https://en.tabitabigujo.com/appeal/taiken-experience/foodsamples/)).

### Do I need to book a food sample workshop in Gujo Hachiman?

Yes. Sample Village Iwasaki requires a reservation, and Sample Kobo takes bookings by phone or online ([Gifu Prefecture tourism](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/spot/detail_1156.html); [Sample Kobo](https://samplekobo.com/)).

### How far is the food sample workshop from Gujo Hachiman Station?

Sample Village Iwasaki is about 5 minutes on foot, and Sample Kobo about 20 minutes (sources above).

### Is Sample Village Iwasaki open every day?

No. Gifu Prefecture's tourism site lists it as closed on Tuesdays, and Iwasaki's own site adds December 29–January 5 and a few set days (sources above).
