Hida beef (*Hida-gyu*) is the wagyu brand of Gifu Prefecture, and Takayama is the easiest place for a first-time visitor to try it. You will see it in many forms: as sushi in the old town, on skewers, inside steamed buns, grilled on a magnolia leaf with miso, and served as sukiyaki or yakiniku at a table.

This guide explains what the name officially means, how the grades work, the main styles you will find, and how to choose between snacking on the street and sitting down for a meal. It does not recommend specific restaurants. Prices and menus change often, so check them at each shop.

If you are still planning the trip itself, start with our [Takayama guide](takayama-from-nagoya.html).

## What counts as Hida beef

"Hida beef" is a protected brand name with set rules, not just any beef from the Hida area. According to the [Takayama city tourism site's Hida beef page](https://www.hida.jp/english/localspeciality/food/4000195.html), Hida beef is meat from Japanese Black cattle (*kuroge wagyu*) raised in Gifu Prefecture for at least 14 months (as of October 2026). Two grading conditions must also be met (as of October 2026):

- a **yield grade of A or B**, and
- a **meat quality grade of 5, 4 or 3**, as graded by the Japan Meat Grading Association.

The same page says the beef must be confirmed by the Hida Beef Brand Promotion Council (*Hida-gyu Meigara Suishin Kyogikai*). Beef from the same cattle that does not meet these grades is sold as **"Hida wagyu"** instead (as of October 2026).

Gifu Prefecture's livestock division sets out the same idea in a [public response on the definition of Hida beef](https://www.pref.gifu.lg.jp/uploaded/attachment/151761.pdf). It lists four conditions (as of October 2026):

1. the animal spent the longest part of its life in Gifu Prefecture;
2. the producer is a farm registered with the Hida Beef Brand Promotion Council;
3. it is a Japanese Black fattened for 14 months or more;
4. its grade is 3 or higher.

So when you see "Hida wagyu" on a sign, that is not a typo. It is a separate label for beef that falls outside the Hida beef grade rules.

## A short background

The [Takayama tourism page](https://www.hida.jp/english/localspeciality/food/4000195.html) traces the brand back to 1981, when a farmer came across a bull named Yasufuku (as of October 2026). It says Yasufuku sired 39,000 offspring in his lifetime (as of October 2026). The [Gifu Prefecture tourism site](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/article/detail_5.html) says the "Hida beef" brand began in 1988, and that more than 90% of Hida beef cattle raised today carry Yasufuku's meat quality (as of October 2026).

The same Gifu page says meat quality is judged on five levels. It adds that grades 4 and 5 make up about 90% of Hida beef, and grade 5 alone about 70% (as of October 2026).

## How to read the grades on a menu

The official definition uses two separate scales:

- **Yield grade (a letter).** Hida beef must be A or B.
- **Meat quality grade (a number).** The [Gifu Prefecture tourism site](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/article/detail_5.html) says quality is judged on five levels (as of October 2026). The [Takayama tourism page](https://www.hida.jp/english/localspeciality/food/4000195.html) describes Hida beef quality in terms of marbling, luster, color, texture and smell (as of October 2026). Hida beef must be 3, 4 or 5.

Shops and menus sometimes show a grade with the beef. If you are not sure what a label means, or whether the dish is "Hida beef" or "Hida wagyu," ask the staff before you order.

**Cuts.** The [Takayama tourism page](https://www.hida.jp/english/localspeciality/food/4000195.html) mentions marbling across the loin, flank, shoulder and round. It names sirloin as one of the finest steaks and says filet steaks are also available (as of October 2026). The [Gifu Prefecture tourism feature](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/article/gifu-hidagyu/top/) mentions loin (*rosu*), short rib (*karubi*) and offal (*horumon*) in the context of grilling (as of October 2026). Useful menu words:

- **Rosu (ロース):** loin
- **Sirloin (サーロイン):** a premium steak cut
- **Hire (ヒレ):** filet / tenderloin
- **Karubi (カルビ):** short rib, common for yakiniku
- **Horumon (ホルモン):** offal

## Street-food styles

The [Gifu Prefecture page on Takayama's old townscape](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/spot/detail_1209.html) lists Hida beef nigiri, skewers and Hida beef buns among the snacks you can eat while you explore the area (as of October 2026). The [Gifu Prefecture Hida beef feature](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/article/gifu-hidagyu/top/) describes the main styles (as of October 2026):

- **Sushi (nigiri).** Served either raw or lightly seared (*aburi*).
- **Skewers (kushiyaki).** Cut into bite-sized pieces, so they are easy to eat.
- **Hida beef buns (*Hida-gyu man*).** Steamed buns packed with Hida beef.
- **Croquettes (korokke).** The page notes that the sweetness of the potato pairs well with the beef.

[Visit Gifu's Hida beef page](https://visitgifu.com/specials-of-gifu/hida-beef/) also mentions beef sushi with seared beef on rice, steak on a stick, beef buns, croquettes and burgers in Takayama (as of October 2026).

**Pros of street food:** small portions, no booking, and a chance to try several styles in one walk.
**Cons:** limited choice of cuts, few or no seats, and queues at busy times.

## Sit-down styles

For a full meal, the [Gifu Prefecture feature](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/article/gifu-hidagyu/top/) describes several table dishes (as of October 2026):

- **Hoba miso.** A Hida local dish where miso is placed on a dried magnolia (*hoba*) leaf and grilled with green onion and mushrooms. The [Gifu tourism feature on Hida beef](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/article/detail_5.html) says dishes that combine hoba miso with Hida beef are popular (as of October 2026).
- **Yakiniku.** You grill the meat yourself at the table. The feature notes that buying loin, short rib and offal from a butcher to grill on a hot plate is a local favorite.
- **Sukiyaki.** The feature recommends coating the beef in egg as you eat it.
- **Shabu-shabu.** Thin slices swished in hot broth. The feature says they are ready to eat once they turn pale pink.
- **Steak.** The feature suggests trying it with coarse salt and wasabi as well as sauce.

The [Takayama tourism page](https://www.hida.jp/english/localspeciality/food/4000195.html) also lists sukiyaki, shabu-shabu and yakiniku as uses for thinly sliced Hida beef (as of October 2026).

**Pros of sitting down:** a wider choice of cuts and grades, time to compare, and a warm meal in cold months.
**Cons:** higher cost than a snack, and some places may ask for a reservation, so check ahead.

## Budget tips

This guide does not quote prices, because they vary by shop, cut and grade and change often. A few general points:

- Street snacks are the cheapest way to taste Hida beef. A skewer or a piece of nigiri is a small, single-item purchase.
- At a sit-down meal, the cut and grade usually affect the price, so compare the menu before ordering.
- If a shop offers set meals, compare them with ordering dishes one by one.
- Check whether a menu says "Hida beef" or "Hida wagyu." The [official definition](https://www.hida.jp/english/localspeciality/food/4000195.html) treats them as different labels.

## Etiquette for street snacks

Takayama's tourism site has a [Manners page](https://www.hida.jp/english/traveltips/practicalguide/4000216.html) for visitors. It says littering, such as dropping cigarette butts and empty cans, is prohibited throughout Takayama City. It also says an offender who ignores a ranger's instructions can be fined 1,000 yen (as of October 2026). The same page says smoking is restricted to designated areas to protect the town's wooden buildings, and asks visitors not to step into the road to take photos (as of October 2026).

In practice, keep skewer sticks, napkins and wrappers with you until you find a bin, or ask the shop that sold the food whether it takes them back. If a shop asks customers to eat at the counter or in a set area, follow that request.

## FAQ

Beef from the same cattle that does not meet these grades is sold as Hida wagyu.

### What grade does beef need to be called Hida beef?

It needs a yield grade of A or B and a meat quality grade of 3, 4 or 5, as graded by the Japan Meat Grading Association (as of October 2026, [Takayama tourism page](https://www.hida.jp/english/localspeciality/food/4000195.html)).
