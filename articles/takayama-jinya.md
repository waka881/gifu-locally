Takayama Jinya is the former government office from which the Edo shogunate ruled Hida Province. In 1692 the shogunate took direct control of Hida, and its appointed officials governed from here for 176 years until 1868; the building was used as a local government office until 1969. Of about 60 such offices at the end of the shogunate, this is the only one whose main building survives ([Takayama Jinya](https://jinya.gifu.jp/en/)). It is a nationally designated historic site.

## What is there to see inside Takayama Jinya?

The official highlights include ([Takayama Jinya](https://jinya.gifu.jp/en/highlight/)):

| Highlight | What it is |
|---|---|
| Ohiroma (reception hall) | The largest space: three connected rooms, 49 tatami mats in all, used for major annual events |
| Oshirasu (courtroom) | Where investigations and judgments took place; the Jinya has two |
| Onkura (rice storehouse) | Moved from Takayama Castle in 1695; one of Japan's oldest and largest Edo-period earthen-walled granaries, with a thatched roof built without nails |
| Arashiyama no Ma | The chief official's living room, with a tea room behind |
| Seigaiha | A wave pattern painted in the entrance alcove (the one on display is a restoration; the original is kept in the storehouse) |
| Mamuki rabbits | Rabbit-shaped covers on the nail heads of the beams, also believed to protect against fire |

## Is there an English guide at Takayama Jinya?

Yes, and it's free. The English **summary guide** takes about 30–40 minutes for groups of 1 to 15; you must enter at least 2 hours before closing. Book at the ticket counter or by phone on +81-577-32-0643 (8:30–17:00). Guides are limited, so your preferred time may not be available, and small groups may be combined ([Takayama Jinya](https://jinya.gifu.jp/en/guide/guidetour/)).

## How do you get to Takayama Jinya?

Walk: it is about 10 minutes from JR Takayama Station, and the Takayama Nohi Bus Center, where highway buses from Nagoya, Kanazawa, Toyama, Osaka and Shinjuku arrive, is also about 10 minutes away ([Takayama Jinya, access](https://jinya.gifu.jp/en/guide/access/)). There is no car park of its own; the nearest city car park, Plaza Jinya, has 20 spaces about 1 minute away (same source). Getting to Takayama: see [from Nagoya](takayama-from-nagoya.html), [from Tokyo](tokyo-to-takayama.html), [from Osaka or Kyoto](osaka-kyoto-to-takayama.html) or [from Kanazawa](takayama-to-kanazawa-bus.html).

## When is the best time to visit Takayama Jinya?

Go in the morning. The Jinya-mae morning market is held right outside every day until noon ([Hida Takayama Tourism](https://www.hida.jp/english/touristattractions/takayamacity/historyandculture/4000162.html)), so you can see the market and then go in when it opens at 8:45. Avoid December 29–January 3, when it is closed; see [Takayama at New Year](takayama-new-year.html). In winter it closes at 16:30, so plan earlier; see [Takayama in winter](takayama-in-winter.html).

For the rest of your day, see [things to do in Takayama](things-to-do-in-takayama.html), the [morning market guide](takayama-morning-market.html) and [Takayama sake breweries](takayama-sake-breweries.html).

## FAQ

### How much is Takayama Jinya?

¥500 for individuals, ¥450 each for groups of 30 or more, and free for visitors aged 18 and under ([Takayama Jinya](https://jinya.gifu.jp/en/guide/info/), as of November 2026).

### What are Takayama Jinya's opening hours?

8:45–17:00 from April to October and 8:45–16:30 from November to March, with tickets sold until 30 minutes before closing; closed December 29–January 3 (same source).

### Is there a free English tour of Takayama Jinya?

Yes. The free English summary guide takes about 30–40 minutes; book at the ticket counter or by phone, and enter at least 2 hours before closing ([Takayama Jinya](https://jinya.gifu.jp/en/guide/guidetour/)).

### How far is Takayama Jinya from Takayama Station?

About 10 minutes on foot from JR Takayama Station ([Takayama Jinya](https://jinya.gifu.jp/en/guide/access/)).
