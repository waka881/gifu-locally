Walk into a neighborhood coffee shop in Gifu before late morning, order a coffee, and something else arrives with it: toast, maybe a boiled egg, sometimes a small salad. You didn't order it, and you won't pay extra for it. This is **morning service** (モーニング, *mōningu*), and Gifu takes it more seriously than almost anywhere in Japan.

## Why Gifu?

Gifu City households spend more on coffee shops than any other prefectural capital or major city in Japan. According to Japan's national Family Income and Expenditure Survey for 2023, Gifu City ranked first for the fourth year in a row ([Gifu City](https://www.city.gifu.lg.jp/_res/projects/default_project/_page_/001/005/752/cafe_ura.pdf)).

Morning service is shared with neighboring Aichi Prefecture, but in Gifu it is less of a tourist attraction and more of a daily routine. You'll be sitting next to retirees reading the paper, not other travelers.

## How it works

1. Go in the morning. Most shops run morning service until around 10 or 11 a.m., but each shop sets its own hours.
2. Order a drink. The food comes with it.
3. Some shops let you choose a set (toast, rice ball, or something sweet). Point at the menu if you're unsure.
4. Pay at the register when you leave. Many small shops are cash only, so carry some yen.

## A few tips

- **Don't expect English menus.** A photo menu or pointing works fine.
- **Weekday mornings are calmer.** Weekends can mean a short wait at popular shops.
- **It's a sit-down habit.** Take your time; nobody will rush you.

## Where to stay for an early start

Staying near JR Gifu Station puts you within walking distance of downtown coffee shops and makes it easy to continue to the Nagara River, the Mt. Kinka Ropeway, or a day trip.

## Make a day of it

Pair a slow morning with a ride up the Mt. Kinka Ropeway (Gifu Castle's keep is closed for renovation until late 2027), or head to the old Kawaramachi district along the Nagara River.
