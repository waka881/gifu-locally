Gifu has two hot spring destinations that international visitors most often weigh up: Gero Onsen on the Hida River in southern Gifu, and Okuhida Onsen-go high in the Northern Alps above Takayama. They suit quite different trips. This guide compares them using figures from the local tourism associations, JR Central and Nohi Bus.

## Gero vs Okuhida at a glance

| | Gero Onsen | Okuhida Onsen-go |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | One hot spring town on the Hida River, with about 50 ryokan and hotels | Five hot spring areas in the mountains of Takayama City, at about 800–1,300 m |
| From Nagoya | Limited Express Hida direct to Gero Station; limited express charge ¥2,390 (reserved seat) plus base fare | About 3 h 30 min or more by limited express and local bus |
| From Takayama | About 45–60 min by JR train | About 58 min to Hirayu Onsen (¥1,600) or about 1 h 35–45 min to Shinhotaka Ropeway (¥2,200) by Nohi Bus |

## What is the difference between Gero and Okuhida?

**Gero Onsen** is a single, compact hot spring town. The [Gifu Prefecture tourism site](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/article/detail_9.html) says about 50 ryokan and hotels line the Hida River, and it counts nine foot baths around town. The [Gero Onsen Tourism Association](https://www.gero-spa.com/onsen/) describes Gero as one of Japan's "three famous hot springs", alongside Arima and Kusatsu (as of October 2026).

**Okuhida Onsen-go** is a group of five hot spring areas spread along mountain valleys: Hirayu, Fukuji, Shin-Hirayu, Tochio and Shinhotaka. The [Okuhida tourism association](https://www.okuhida.or.jp/en/) says the area sits at about 800 to 1,300 m, surrounded by 3,000 m-class Northern Alps peaks, and that its number of open-air baths is one of the largest in Japan (as of October 2026). It is also a base for Kamikochi and Mount Norikura.

In short, Gero is a hot spring town you can walk around in a few hours. Okuhida is a mountain area where the inns and their open-air baths are the main reason to go.

## How does the water compare?

- **Gero:** an alkaline simple spring with a pH of 9.2 ([Gero Onsen Tourism Association](https://www.gero-spa.com/onsen/)), described as colourless and transparent ([Gifu Prefecture tourism site](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/article/detail_9.html)).
- **Okuhida:** over 100 hot springs fed by Yake-dake, an active volcano, with a flow of about 37,000 litres per minute, which the association says is the third most abundant in Japan ([Okuhida tourism association](https://www.okuhida.or.jp/en/)). Spring types vary by area and inn, including simple, bicarbonate, sulphur and chloride springs ([Okuhida hot springs page](https://okuhida.or.jp/en/hot-springs/)).

## Which is easier to reach?

**Gero is much easier.** It is a stop on the JR Takayama Line, and the Limited Express Hida runs from Nagoya Station to Gero Station. JR Central lists the ticketless limited express charge from Nagoya to Gero at ¥2,390 for an adult reserved seat, one way in the normal season, plus the base fare ([JR Central press release](https://jr-central.co.jp/news/release/_pdf/000044393.pdf), as of July 16, 2025). Ticket options and discounts are in [Nagoya to Gero Onsen](nagoya-to-gero-onsen.html). From Takayama, JR Central gives the train time to Gero as about 45–60 minutes ([JR Central](https://global.jr-central.co.jp/en/goldenroute/itineraries/takayama-gero-craftsmanship-onsen/)).

**Okuhida needs a bus from Takayama.** The Okuhida tourism association gives the trip from Nagoya by train as about 3 hours 30 minutes or more, by limited express and then a local bus ([Okuhida access](https://okuhida.or.jp/en/access/)). The Nohi Bus Shinhotaka Line leaves from Takayama Bus Terminal, roughly hourly ([Nohi Bus](https://www.nouhibus.co.jp/route_bus/shinhotaka-line-en/), as of October 2026):

| Takayama Bus Terminal to | Time (from timetable) | Adult fare one way |
|---|---|---|
| Hirayu Onsen | About 58 min | ¥1,600 |
| Fukuji Onsen | — | ¥1,800 |
| Shin-Hirayu Onsen | — | ¥1,850 |
| Tochio Onsen | — | ¥1,930 |
| Shinhotaka Ropeway | About 1 h 35–45 min | ¥2,200 |

Nohi Bus warns that buses can be badly delayed by snow or traffic in Takayama, so allow extra time in winter and during the autumn leaf season.

## Can you visit without staying overnight?

**Gero: yes, easily.** Public baths such as Shirasagi-no-yu and Sachi-no-yu charge ¥470 for adults, Kur Garden Rotenburo charges ¥800, and the Yumeguri Tegata pass gives three day-use baths at member ryokan for ¥2,500 ([Gero Onsen Tourism Association](https://www.gero-spa.com/spot/detail_50.html), as of October 2026). Hours and closing days are in our [Gero Onsen guide](gero-onsen-guide.html).

**Okuhida: possible, but it is a long day.** The tourism association lists a few day-use baths ([Okuhida hot springs page](https://okuhida.or.jp/en/hot-springs/), as of October 2026):

- **Hirayu no Mori** in Hirayu Onsen, with 16 open-air baths across the men's and women's sides.
- **Hirayu no Yu**, an outdoor bath next to the Hirayu Folk Museum, entered for a small donation.
- **Kojin no Yu** in Tochio Onsen, an unstaffed riverside rock bath, for a donation of about ¥300, open until 22:00.
- **Shinhotaka no Yu** near Shinhotaka, closed in winter.

With up to about 1¾ hours each way on the bus from Takayama to the farther areas, most visitors get more from Okuhida by staying a night at an inn with its own open-air bath.

## What else is there to do?

**In Gero:** walk between the free foot baths, and visit Gero Onsen Gassho Village, an open-air museum of thatched farmhouses, ¥800 for adults ([Gero Onsen Tourism Association](https://www.gero-spa.com/spot/detail_12.html), as of October 2026).

**In Okuhida:** ride the Shinhotaka Ropeway, which the operator calls Japan's only two-storey gondola, to a top station at 2,156 m. The adult round trip is ¥3,800, and it is listed as open year-round, subject to weather ([Shinhotaka Ropeway](https://shinhotaka-ropeway.jp/en/price/), as of October 2026). The area is also a base for hiking, and for Kamikochi and Mount Norikura ([Okuhida tourism association](https://www.okuhida.or.jp/en/)). Our [Shinhotaka Ropeway guide](shinhotaka-ropeway.html) has tickets, buses and winter tips, and [Kamikochi from Takayama](kamikochi-from-takayama.html) covers the seasonal bus from Hirayu Onsen.

## So which should you choose?

**Choose Gero Onsen** if you are based in Nagoya, want a simple day trip or one night, or are travelling between Nagoya and Takayama by train and want to break the journey.

**Choose Okuhida Onsen-go** if you are already going to Takayama, want mountain views from an open-air bath, and can stay at least one night.

**Do both** on a longer Hida trip: Gero on the way up from Nagoya, Takayama for a night, then Okuhida. See our [Takayama 2-day itinerary](takayama-2-day-itinerary.html) and [where to stay in Takayama](where-to-stay-takayama.html).

## FAQ

### Is Gero or Okuhida better for a day trip from Nagoya?

Gero. It has its own station on the Limited Express Hida line from Nagoya, and public baths that charge ¥470 for adults ([Gero Onsen Tourism Association](https://www.gero-spa.com/spot/detail_3.html), as of October 2026). Okuhida takes about 3 hours 30 minutes or more from Nagoya by train and bus, according to the Okuhida tourism association.

### How do you get from Takayama to Okuhida Onsen?

Take the Nohi Bus Shinhotaka Line from Takayama Bus Terminal. It takes about 58 minutes to Hirayu Onsen (¥1,600 for adults) and about 1 hour 35–45 minutes to the Shinhotaka Ropeway (¥2,200) ([Nohi Bus](https://www.nouhibus.co.jp/route_bus/shinhotaka-line-en/), as of October 2026).

### What are the five hot spring areas of Okuhida?

Hirayu, Fukuji, Shin-Hirayu, Tochio and Shinhotaka ([Okuhida hot springs page](https://okuhida.or.jp/en/hot-springs/), as of October 2026).

### Is Gero Onsen one of Japan's three famous hot springs?

The Gero Onsen Tourism Association describes Gero as one of Japan's "three famous hot springs" (*Nihon Sanmeisen*), with Arima in Hyogo and Kusatsu in Gunma ([Gero Onsen Tourism Association](https://www.gero-spa.com/onsen/)).
