Mobile data does a lot of work on a trip to Gifu. You use it for train times between Nagoya and Takayama, bus tickets to Shirakawa-go, maps on the Nakasendo trail and translation in small restaurants. Most first-time visitors pick one of three options: an **eSIM**, a **pocket WiFi** router or a **physical prepaid SIM**. This guide compares them, shows you how to check coverage in the mountains for yourself, and walks through setting up an eSIM.

## The short answer

- **Your phone is unlocked and supports eSIM:** a data-only travel eSIM is usually the least hassle. You install it before you fly and switch it on when you land.
- **Several people want to share one connection, or someone's phone has no eSIM:** consider pocket WiFi.
- **Your phone takes only a physical SIM:** a prepaid data SIM is the remaining option.

Whichever you choose, look up your route on the coverage map of the network it uses (see below). Don't assume every valley and trail has a signal.

## What to know about coverage in rural Gifu

The northern part of Gifu Prefecture is mountainous. Takayama, the Okuhida hot-spring villages, Shirakawa-go and the Nakasendo post towns of Magome and Tsumago all sit in or near the Japanese Alps. In terrain like this, signal can depend on where you are standing: a valley floor, a tunnel, a mountain trail or the inside of a thick-walled building.

Your eSIM, SIM or pocket WiFi runs on one of Japan's mobile networks, so that network's coverage is what matters. The main carriers each publish an official coverage map:

- **NTT docomo:** the [docomo service area page](https://www.docomo.ne.jp/english/area/) links to 5G and 4G (LTE) area maps. The detailed search tools are in Japanese only.
- **SoftBank:** the [SoftBank service area map](https://www.softbank.jp/mobile/network/area-map/) shows SoftBank 5G, 4G LTE and 4G coverage (in Japanese).
- **au (KDDI):** the [au area page in English](https://www.au.com/english/mobile/area/) links to a nationwide service area map. Many of the linked pages are in Japanese only.

**How to check your route:**

1. Find out which network your product uses. It is usually listed on the product page. For example, the Klook Japan eSIM page lists SoftBank 5G and DOCOMO 4G LTE options.
2. Open that carrier's map and find the places you plan to visit. Japanese place names can help on Japanese-only pages: Gifu Prefecture (岐阜県), Takayama (高山市), Shirakawa village (白川村) and Nakatsugawa (中津川市), where Magome is.
3. Check the specific spots you'll be at, not just the town centre: trailheads, your ryokan and bus stops.
4. Treat the map as a guide. Download offline maps and save your tickets and bookings on the phone before heading into the mountains.

If you plan to walk the Nakasendo between Magome and Tsumago, read our [Magome–Tsumago walk guide](magome-tsumago-walk.html) for route details. Before a snowy-season visit, see the [Gifu winter guide](gifu-winter-guide.html).

## Option 1: eSIM

An eSIM is a digital SIM built into your phone. A travel eSIM adds a data line alongside your home number, so you don't remove any card.

**Check your phone first:**

- **iPhone:** Apple says eSIM needs an iPhone XS, XS Max, XR or later, with exceptions for some models sold in mainland China, Hong Kong and Macao ([Apple: Set up eSIM on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118669)). Your iPhone must also not be locked to a carrier. Go to **Settings > General > About**. If Carrier Lock says "No SIM restrictions", the phone is unlocked ([Apple: Use eSIM while traveling internationally](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118227)).
- **Android / Pixel:** Google's Pixel help explains how to add an eSIM under **Settings > Network & internet > SIMs > Add SIM > Set up an eSIM** ([Google: Set up a new eSIM](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/16115470?hl=en)). For other Android brands, check the maker's support page and the eSIM seller's list of compatible devices.

## How to set up an eSIM (iPhone and Android)

Apple notes that you need a Wi-Fi connection while setting up a travel eSIM ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118227)). Installing it at home, before you leave, is the easiest approach.

**On iPhone** ([Apple: Set up eSIM on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118669)):

1. Open the Camera app and scan the QR code from your eSIM provider, then tap **Cellular Plan Detected**. If the code is in an email or web page, on iOS 17.4 or later you can press and hold it and choose **Add eSIM**.
2. Tap **Continue**, then **Add Cellular Plan**. Enter a confirmation code if asked.
3. No QR code? Go to **Settings > Cellular > Add Cellular Plan > Enter Details Manually**.
4. When you arrive in Japan, go to **Settings > Cellular > Cellular Data** and choose the travel eSIM ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118227)). Then turn on data roaming for that line, if your provider's instructions say to.

Apple also notes that your home line can still bring roaming charges while the travel eSIM carries your data. Check your home carrier's roaming settings before you go ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118227)).

**On Google Pixel** ([Google](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/16115470?hl=en)):

1. Go to **Settings > Network & internet > SIMs > Add SIM > Set up an eSIM**.
2. Follow the on-screen instructions to add the eSIM from your provider.
3. After you land, set the travel eSIM as your mobile-data SIM and follow your provider's instructions on data roaming.

## Option 2: Pocket WiFi

A pocket WiFi is a small portable router. Everyone in your group connects their phone, tablet or laptop to it over Wi-Fi.

- **Pros:** one connection for several devices, and no changes to anyone's phone settings.
- **Cons:** it is a second device to carry, charge and keep with you. If the person holding it walks off, everyone else loses their connection. Most rental services also need you to collect and return it.

On long days, such as a day trip from Nagoya to [Shirakawa-go](shirakawa-go-from-nagoya.html) or a full day in [Takayama](takayama-from-nagoya.html), bring a power bank so the router lasts.

## Option 3: Physical prepaid SIM

A prepaid data SIM card goes into your phone's SIM slot in place of your home card. It suits phones that don't support eSIM. The phone must still be unlocked, and you'll need to keep your home SIM card safe until you swap it back. Check which network the SIM uses and look it up on that carrier's map, as with any other option.

## Which should you choose for Gifu?

- **Solo or couple, unlocked eSIM phones, rail-and-bus trip** (Nagoya, Takayama, Shirakawa-go): an eSIM keeps things simple. Our [Nagoya day trips](nagoya-day-trips.html) guide covers routes you can plan this way.
- **Family or group, mixed devices:** pocket WiFi lets everyone share one plan.
- **Older or locked phone:** first check whether your home carrier can unlock it or offers a Japan roaming plan. Otherwise, a pocket WiFi doesn't depend on your phone at all.
- **Hiking the Nakasendo or visiting remote onsen in Okuhida:** whichever option you choose, check the coverage map for that network and keep offline maps as a backup.

## FAQ

### Can I keep my home phone number while using a Japan eSIM?

Yes, on dual-SIM phones. Apple explains that a travel eSIM can carry data while your home line stays on, for example for iMessage and FaceTime. Your home line may still bring roaming charges ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118227)).

### Will my eSIM work in Shirakawa-go or Okuhida?

It depends on the network your eSIM uses and where exactly you are. Check that carrier's official coverage map ([docomo](https://www.docomo.ne.jp/english/area/), [SoftBank](https://www.softbank.jp/mobile/network/area-map/), [au](https://www.au.com/english/mobile/area/)) for each place on your route.

### When should I install the eSIM?

Before you fly, while you're on Wi-Fi at home. On the Klook eSIM, validity starts when the eSIM connects to a network overseas, according to its product page. Check the terms of whichever eSIM you buy.

### Does a data-only eSIM give me a Japanese phone number?

The Klook Japan eSIM is described as data-only. For calls, use internet-based apps, or keep your home line on for calls (roaming charges may apply).
