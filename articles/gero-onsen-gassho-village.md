Gero Onsen is best known for its hot springs, but on the hill above town is a small village of thatched farmhouses. This guide uses the Gassho Village's own site and the Gero Onsen Tourism Association.

## Gero Onsen Gassho Village at a glance

| | Details |
|---|---|
| What it is | Open-air museum of 10 gassho-style farmhouses, moved from Shirakawa-go and other areas |
| Adult admission | ¥800 (senior high school and older) |
| Children | ¥400 (elementary and junior high) |
| Hours | 8:30–17:00, last entry 16:30; 9:00–16:00 on December 31–January 2 |
| Closed | Open all year (may close in special circumstances) |
| From JR Gero Station | About 20 min on foot, or about 6 min by Nohi Bus to "Gassho-mura" |

*Sources: [Gero Onsen Gassho Village](https://www.gero-gassho.jp/lg_en/); [Gero Onsen Tourism Association](https://www.gero-spa.com/spot/detail_12.html), as of November 2026.*

## What can you see at the Gassho Village?

Ten gassho-zukuri farmhouses moved from places such as Shirakawa-go. The highlight is the Former Oto Family House, a nationally designated Important Tangible Folk Cultural Property, where you can see how people used to live. The site also has the Iwasaki House folk museum, craft workshops such as ceramics and patterned washi paper, a restaurant serving Hida cuisine, and a hilltop forest of cherry and maple trees ([Gero Onsen Gassho Village](https://www.gero-gassho.jp/lg_en/); [Gero Onsen Tourism Association](https://www.gero-spa.com/spot/detail_12.html), as of November 2026).

## How do you get there from Gero Station?

Walk about 20 minutes, or take the Nohi Bus bound for Gero Koryu Kaikan for about 6 minutes and get off at "Gassho-mura", right by the entrance ([Gero Onsen Tourism Association](https://www.gero-spa.com/spot/detail_12.html)). For the train to Gero, see [Nagoya to Gero Onsen](nagoya-to-gero-onsen.html), [Takayama to Gero Onsen](takayama-to-gero-onsen.html) and [Osaka or Kyoto to Gero Onsen](osaka-kyoto-to-gero-onsen.html).

## Is it worth it, or should you go to Shirakawa-go?

If you will reach Shirakawa-go, the real village comes first: it is part of the UNESCO World Heritage site Historic Villages of Shirakawa-go and Gokayama ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/734/)), and the Tourist Association suggests 2–3 hours there ([Shirakawa-go Tourist Association](https://shirakawa-go.gr.jp/en/faq/5/)). If your trip is Nagoya and Gero only, the Gassho Village is the easy way to see thatched houses up close in an hour or two. Compare in [Is Shirakawa-go worth visiting?](is-shirakawa-go-worth-visiting.html), and see [Hida Folk Village](hida-folk-village.html) for the similar museum in Takayama.

## FAQ

### How much is Gero Onsen Gassho Village?

¥800 for adults and ¥400 for elementary and junior high students ([Gero Onsen Gassho Village](https://www.gero-gassho.jp/lg_en/), as of November 2026).

### What are the opening hours?

8:30–17:00 every day, with last entry at 16:30; 9:00–16:00 on December 31–January 2 (same source).

### How far is the Gassho Village from Gero Station?

About 20 minutes on foot, or about 6 minutes by Nohi Bus to the "Gassho-mura" stop ([Gero Onsen Tourism Association](https://www.gero-spa.com/spot/detail_12.html)).

### Are the houses from Shirakawa-go?

The 10 gassho-style farmhouses were moved from Shirakawa-go and other areas (same source).
