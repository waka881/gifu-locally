Takayama and Kanazawa are often compared because they sit on either side of Shirakawa-go and both have preserved old districts. They feel quite different: Takayama is a small town in the mountains of Gifu, Kanazawa a city in Ishikawa Prefecture. This guide compares them using official tourism, garden, rail and bus sources.

## What is Takayama like?

Takayama's draw is its compact old town: the Sanmachi merchant quarter about 12 minutes on foot from JR Takayama Station, two daily morning markets that close at noon, seven historic sake breweries and Takayama Jinya, the only surviving main building of an Edo-period provincial government office ([Takayama Jinya](https://jinya.gifu.jp/en/)). It is also the base for Okuhida's mountain hot springs. See [things to do in Takayama](things-to-do-in-takayama.html) and [Takayama Jinya](takayama-jinya.html).

## What is Kanazawa like?

Kanazawa is a city whose best-known sights are a garden and its teahouse districts. **Kenrokuen** costs ¥320 for adults and opens 7:00–18:00 from March 1 to October 15 and 8:00–17:00 from October 16 to the end of February, with free early entry through two gates ([Kenrokuen](https://shiro-niwa.pref.ishikawa.lg.jp/kenrokuen-garden/e/info.html)). The **Higashi Chaya district**, a former teahouse quarter designated an Important Preservation District for Groups of Traditional Buildings, is about 7 minutes by bus from Kanazawa Station; Visit Kanazawa also says the city has a 99% share of Japan's gold leaf market ([Visit Kanazawa](https://visitkanazawa.jp/en/higashi)).

Unsure about Takayama itself? See [Is Takayama worth visiting?](is-takayama-worth-visiting.html)

## Which should you choose?

- **Choose Takayama** if you want a small, walkable town, a morning market, sake and Hida beef, onsen nearby, or are coming from Nagoya.
- **Choose Kanazawa** if you want a famous garden, teahouse districts and city hotels, or are coming from Tokyo on the Hokuriku Shinkansen.
- **Do both** if you have three days or more: they are linked by the bus via Shirakawa-go, so the thatched village fits between them.

## How do you visit both Takayama and Kanazawa?

Nohi Bus runs reserved buses from the Takayama Nohi Bus Center to Kanazawa Station via Shirakawa-go, about 2 hours 15 minutes for ¥4,200; to stop in Shirakawa-go, buy two tickets (¥2,800 each leg) and use a coin locker at the bus terminal. See [Takayama to Kanazawa by bus](takayama-to-kanazawa-bus.html) for the timetable. Nohi Bus says fares and timetables will be revised from December 1, 2026 ([Nohi Bus](https://www.nouhibus.co.jp/route_bus/shirakawago_en/)), so check the page for your date.

## FAQ

### Is Takayama or Kanazawa better?

Takayama suits travellers who want a small mountain town with markets and sake; Kanazawa suits those who want Kenrokuen garden and the teahouse districts. They are about 2 hours 15 minutes apart by bus, so many people visit both (sources above).

### How far is Takayama from Kanazawa?

About 2 hours 15 minutes by Nohi Bus via Shirakawa-go, ¥4,200 one way for adults ([Nohi Bus](https://www.nouhibus.co.jp/route_bus/shirakawago_en/), as of November 2026).

### Is Shirakawa-go closer to Takayama or Kanazawa?

Takayama: about 50 minutes by bus, compared with about 1 hour 20 minutes from Kanazawa (same source).

### How long does it take from Tokyo to Kanazawa?

2 hours 28 minutes by Hokuriku Shinkansen ([JR West](https://www.westjr.co.jp/global/en/shinkansen/hokuriku-shinkansen/)).
