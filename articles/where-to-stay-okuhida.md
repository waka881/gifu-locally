Okuhida Onsengo is a group of five hot spring villages in the mountains east of Takayama, at the foot of the Northern Alps. The tourism association says the area has one of the largest numbers of open-air baths in Japan ([Okuhida Onsengo](https://www.okuhida.or.jp/en/hot-springs/)). Which village you pick decides how easy the buses are and what you see from the bath. This guide uses the Okuhida tourism association and Nohi Bus.

## Which Okuhida village should you stay in?

| Village | What it's like | Adult bus fare from Takayama | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hirayu Onsen | Old spa town at the foot of Mt. Norikura; foot baths and the Hirayu no Mori bathhouse (16 open-air baths) | ¥1,600 (about 53–58 min) | Bus connections, Kamikochi |
| Fukuji Onsen | Small mountain village spring, the "Emperor's Spring" of legend | ¥1,800 | A quiet, rustic stay |
| Shin-Hirayu Onsen | Highland town spread over a plateau; Enku Buddha statues at Zentsuji Temple | ¥1,850 | A middle point with more inns |
| Tochio Onsen | Riverside spa town known for home cooking and stream fishing; Kojin no Yu open-air bath by the Kamata River | ¥1,930 | Riverside, simple inns |
| Shinhotaka Onsen | Open-air bath resort on a plateau facing the Northern Alps; trailhead for Mt. Yari and Mt. Hotaka | ¥2,200 (Shinhotaka Ropeway stop) | The ropeway, mountain views, hikers |

*Sources: [Okuhida Onsengo, hot springs](https://www.okuhida.or.jp/en/hot-springs/); [Nohi Bus, Shinhotaka line](https://www.nouhibus.co.jp/route_bus/shinhotaka-line-en/), as of November 2026. Bus times to Hirayu from the timetable; buses can be badly delayed by snow.*

## Why stay in Hirayu Onsen?

Hirayu is the transport hub of Okuhida. Buses from Takayama take about 53–58 minutes ([Nohi Bus](https://www.nouhibus.co.jp/route_bus/shinhotaka-line-en/)), the Matsumoto–Takayama bus stops here ([Matsumoto to Takayama](matsumoto-to-takayama.html)), and in season it is where you change for Kamikochi, whose buses ran from April 17 to November 15 in 2026 ([Kamikochi from Takayama](kamikochi-from-takayama.html)). For baths, the association lists Hirayu no Mori with 16 open-air baths, plus foot baths around the village ([Okuhida Onsengo](https://www.okuhida.or.jp/en/hot-springs/)).

## Why stay in Shinhotaka Onsen?

Choose Shinhotaka if you plan to ride the [Shinhotaka Ropeway](shinhotaka-ropeway.html) early, or are hiking. The association describes it as an open-air bath resort facing the Northern Alps. Note that its public mixed open-air bath, Shinhotaka no Yu, closes in winter (same source). Nohi Bus marks several services as running only from April 1 to November 30, so expect fewer buses in winter ([Nohi Bus](https://www.nouhibus.co.jp/route_bus/shinhotaka-line-en/)).

## What about Fukuji, Shin-Hirayu and Tochio?

They sit along the road between Hirayu and Shinhotaka. **Fukuji** is the smallest and most rustic; the association highlights inns with large indoor and stream-side open-air baths. **Shin-Hirayu** is a plateau town with temple history. **Tochio** is a riverside village with Kojin no Yu, a communal open-air bath by the Kamata River that asks a small donation and is open until 22:00 ([Okuhida Onsengo](https://www.okuhida.or.jp/en/hot-springs/)).

## FAQ

### What are the five Okuhida hot spring villages?

Hirayu, Fukuji, Shin-Hirayu, Tochio and Shinhotaka ([Okuhida Onsengo](https://www.okuhida.or.jp/en/hot-springs/)).

### Which Okuhida village is easiest to reach?

Hirayu Onsen: about 53–58 minutes by Nohi Bus from Takayama for ¥1,600, with the Matsumoto bus and the seasonal Kamikochi buses stopping there too ([Nohi Bus](https://www.nouhibus.co.jp/route_bus/shinhotaka-line-en/), as of November 2026).

### How much is the bus from Takayama to Okuhida?

From ¥1,600 to Hirayu Onsen up to ¥2,200 to the Shinhotaka Ropeway, adult one way (same source).

### Can I visit Okuhida in winter?

Yes, but some buses run only from April 1 to November 30, snow can delay buses, and Shinhotaka's public mixed open-air bath closes in winter (sources above). See [Takayama in winter](takayama-in-winter.html).
