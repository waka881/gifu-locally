Gifu City is small enough that you can't really pick a "wrong" place to sleep, but the two main areas feel very different. Around **JR Gifu Station** you get business hotels, trains in every direction and a dense neighborhood of restaurants. Up by the Nagara River, **Nagaragawa Onsen** is a compact hot-spring area of ryokan and riverside hotels, close to Gifu Castle, the old Kawaramachi district and the summer cormorant fishing.

This guide compares the two so you can decide where to stay in Gifu based on how you plan to spend your time. It doesn't rank individual hotels; it explains what each area is like and what to look for when you book.

## The quick answer

- **Stay near JR Gifu Station** if Gifu City is your base for day trips, you are arriving late or leaving early by train, or you want simple, room-only accommodation and the freedom to eat out.
- **Stay at Nagaragawa Onsen** if you want a ryokan-style night with a hot-spring bath and dinner included, a river view, or easy evening access to the cormorant fishing boats and Kawaramachi.

The two areas are linked by frequent city buses, so many visitors do one night in each, or base themselves at the station and visit the river area for an afternoon and evening.

## The JR Gifu Station area

### What it's like

JR Gifu Station is the city's transport hub. Meitetsu Gifu Station, for private-railway trains to Nagoya, is a short walk away, and the bus terminal outside JR Gifu Station is where city buses leave for Gifu Park and the Nagara River. Most of the area's accommodation is modern hotels with Western-style rooms.

Just in front of the station is **Tamamiya**, an izakaya district that JR Central describes as having over 400 restaurants and bars ([JR Central](https://global.jr-central.co.jp/en/onlinebooking/contents/hello_gifu/), as of October 2026). That makes the station area convenient if you'd rather choose a different place for dinner each night than eat a set meal at your hotel. Neighborhood coffee shops serving **morning service**, Gifu's breakfast-with-your-coffee custom, are also easy to find. See our guide to [Gifu's morning service](gifu-morning-service.html).

### Getting around from here

Nagoya is close: JR Nagoya Station is around 20 minutes by train from JR Gifu Station ([Visit Gifu](https://visitgifu.com/how-to-get-to/nagoya/), as of October 2026). That makes the station area a practical base if you're splitting your time between Gifu and Nagoya, or using rail day trips to places further afield. Our [day trips from Nagoya](nagoya-day-trips.html) guide covers options that also work from Gifu.

For the sights in Gifu City itself, the official tourism site gives these bus connections from JR Gifu Station:

- **To Gifu Park and the Mt. Kinka Ropeway:** Gifu Bus "N" routes toward Nagarabashi (route numbers N32–N86) from stops 12 and 13, or the City Loop Line counter-clockwise from stop 11. Get off at "Gifu Kōen / Gifu-jō" (Gifu Park / Gifu Castle). The ride takes about 14–15 minutes and costs ¥250, and the park is a 3-minute walk from the stop ([Gifu Convention & Visitors Bureau](https://www.gifucvb.or.jp/access/city.php), as of October 2026).
- **To Nagarabashi (Kawaramachi and the cormorant fishing boats):** the City Loop Line counter-clockwise from stop 11 takes about 15 minutes to "Nagarabashi-minami / Kawaramachi" and costs ¥250 ([Gifu Convention & Visitors Bureau](https://www.gifucvb.or.jp/access/city.php), as of October 2026).

The same routes also run from stop 4 at Meitetsu Gifu Station ([Gifu Convention & Visitors Bureau](https://www.gifucvb.or.jp/access/city.php), as of October 2026). Gifu's buses accept the local ayuca IC card and nationwide transit IC cards such as manaca ([Gifu City](https://www.city.gifu.lg.jp/foreign/eng/benri/2000148.html), as of October 2026).

### Who it suits

- Rail travelers moving on to other cities, or doing day trips
- Visitors who want room-only rates and prefer to eat out
- Anyone with an early train or a late arrival
- Travelers on a single overnight who want the simplest logistics

### Trade-offs

You won't get a hot-spring bath or a river view at a typical station-area hotel, and the castle, Kawaramachi and the river are a bus ride away rather than a walk.

## Nagaragawa Onsen

### What it's like

Nagaragawa Onsen is a hot-spring area along the clear Nagara River, at the foot of Mt. Kinka where Gifu Castle stands, and most of its inns face the river ([Gifu Convention & Visitors Bureau](https://www.gifucvb.or.jp/sightseeing/detail_kankou.php?eid=00059), as of October 2026). The local hotel cooperative lists 7 member ryokan and hotels, 4 on the north bank and 3 on the south bank ([Gifu Nagaragawa Onsen Hotel Cooperative](https://nagaragawa.org/en/about), as of October 2026). The mix runs from traditional ryokan with mostly Japanese-style tatami rooms to larger hotels with Western-style twin rooms ([Gifu Nagaragawa Onsen Hotel Cooperative](https://nagaragawa.org/en/stay), as of October 2026).

The spring is classified as a simple iron cold mineral spring. The water comes out colorless and turns reddish-brown as it meets the air ([Gifu Convention & Visitors Bureau](https://www.gifucvb.or.jp/sightseeing/detail_kankou.php?eid=00059), as of October 2026). Not every member property has hot-spring baths, so if bathing is the point of your stay, check that the place you book has them. The tourism bureau lists which inns have onsen baths, and some of them also offer day-use bathing to non-guests at set hours ([Gifu Convention & Visitors Bureau](https://www.gifucvb.or.jp/sightseeing/detail_kankou.php?eid=00059), as of October 2026).

The tourism bureau also notes that the area's inns serve local dishes such as fresh ayu (sweetfish), with some special meals needing advance reservation ([Gifu Convention & Visitors Bureau](https://www.gifucvb.or.jp/sightseeing/detail_kankou.php?eid=00059), as of October 2026).

### What's nearby

- **Ukai (cormorant fishing):** the viewing boats leave from beside Nagara Bridge, in the middle of the onsen area. The season runs **May 11 to October 15 each year**, and the fishing starts at 19:45. It doesn't run on one rest day around the mid-autumn full moon or when the river is too high. Shared viewing boats cost ¥4,200 for adults on regular days and ¥5,100 on designated busy days ([Gifu Convention & Visitors Bureau](https://www.gifucvb.or.jp/sightseeing/ukai.php), as of October 2026). The 2026 season has ended, so the next season opens in May 2027. Staying by the river means you can walk back to your room after the boats return.
- **Kawaramachi:** an old riverside merchant port district with latticed houses and storehouses, now full of shops, cafés and restaurants, about a 5-minute walk from the Nagarabashi bus stop ([Visit Gifu](https://visitgifu.com/see-do/kawara-machi-district/), as of October 2026).
- **Gifu Park, the ropeway and Gifu Castle:** the ropeway ride is about 4 minutes, followed by an 8-minute walk to the castle, and an adult round trip costs ¥1,300. Note that the castle keep is closed for renovation from May 19, 2026, with reopening currently planned for late 2027 ([Gifu Convention & Visitors Bureau](https://www.gifucvb.or.jp/sightseeing/gifu-castle.php), as of October 2026). The summit is still reachable by ropeway, except on its scheduled maintenance dates.

### Getting there from the station

Visit Gifu gives the bus ride from Gifu Station to the Nagarabashi or Ukaiya stops as about 20 minutes ([Visit Gifu](https://visitgifu.com/see-do/nagaragawa-hot-springs/), as of October 2026). For the south bank near Kawaramachi, the City Loop Line route to Nagarabashi-minami / Kawaramachi above also works. Properties are spread along both banks, so check your hotel's own access page for the nearest stop. If you're coming by car, guest parking at the hotels and inns is free ([Gifu Nagaragawa Onsen Hotel Cooperative](https://nagaragawa.org/en/access), as of October 2026).

### Who it suits

- Visitors who want a ryokan experience with a bath, a yukata and dinner
- Couples and families making a single special night of their Gifu stop
- Summer visitors planning to see the cormorant fishing
- Anyone who prefers a quiet riverside evening to a busy station district

### Trade-offs

Rates at ryokan often include dinner and breakfast, so they cost more per night than a room-only city hotel. Evenings are quieter, with fewer independent places to eat late. You'll also need a bus back to the station for onward trains, so allow extra time on travel days.

## How to choose: questions to ask before you book

**Do you want meals included?** Ryokan plans usually include a multi-course dinner and breakfast at set times. That's part of the experience, but it ties you to the inn for the evening. If you'd rather explore Tamamiya's restaurants or try a morning service café, a room-only station hotel gives you more freedom.

**Is the onsen important?** If it is, choose Nagaragawa Onsen and confirm the property has hot-spring baths, not just a regular bath. If you only want a soak and not an overnight stay, day-use bathing at some inns is an option ([Gifu Convention & Visitors Bureau](https://www.gifucvb.or.jp/sightseeing/detail_kankou.php?eid=00059), as of October 2026).

**Do you want a river view?** Most inns face the river, but not every room does. If the view matters, look for room types that specify it.

**Tatami or bed?** Many ryokan rooms are Japanese-style, with futons laid out on tatami. If you need a Western bed, check the room type when booking.

**What does the rest of your trip look like?** If tomorrow means an early train to Takayama, Nagoya or beyond, the station area saves time. If Gifu City itself is the highlight, the river area puts you in the middle of it.

## A simple plan that uses both areas

If you have two nights, a common pattern is to arrive at JR Gifu Station, stay one night nearby, and spend the next day on the sights in our [Gifu City in one day](gifu-city-one-day.html) itinerary: breakfast, the ropeway, then Kawaramachi. Then move your bags to Nagaragawa Onsen for an evening by the river, with the cormorant fishing if you're visiting between May and mid-October. With one night, decide on your priority: easy train connections (station) or the onsen and river (Nagaragawa).
