Gero Onsen is a hot spring town on the Hida River in southern Gifu Prefecture. The [Gero Onsen Tourism Association](https://www.gero-spa.com/onsen/) describes it as one of Japan's "three famous hot springs" (*Nihon Sanmeisen*), alongside Arima in Hyogo and Kusatsu in Gunma. It sits on the JR Takayama Line between Nagoya and Takayama, so it works as a day trip from Nagoya or as an overnight stop on the way into the Hida mountains.

This guide covers getting there, where to bathe without staying at an inn, the Yumeguri Tegata bath pass, onsen etiquette, and how to pick a ryokan if you decide to stay.

## What makes Gero's water different

According to the [tourism association's onsen page](https://www.gero-spa.com/onsen/), the water is an alkaline simple spring (*arukari-sei tanjun onsen*) with a pH of 9.2 (as of October 2026). The [Gifu Prefecture tourism site](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/article/detail_9.html) describes it as colorless and transparent. It also says Gero adopted a centralized hot spring management system in 1974, which pipes water from shared tanks to the inns (as of October 2026).

The same Gifu page notes that about 50 ryokan and hotels line the Hida River, and some of them offer day-use bathing (as of October 2026).

## Getting to Gero Onsen from Nagoya

The usual route is the **JR Limited Express Hida** from Nagoya Station along the Takayama Line to **Gero Station**. Hida trains continue on toward Takayama, so check that you get off at Gero.

**Tickets.** On the Hida you pay a base fare plus a limited express charge. JR Central's [July 2025 press release](https://jr-central.co.jp/news/release/_pdf/000044393.pdf) lists the ticketless limited express charge for Nagoya to Gero as **2,390 yen for an adult ordinary reserved seat** and **4,660 yen for the Green Car**, one way in the normal season. This is the charge only, and the base fare is extra (as of July 16, 2025). The same release says that from October 1, 2025, you can book the Hida ticketlessly through JR West's e5489 service. It also says TOICA IC cards became valid at Gero Station for Hida journeys, which covers the base fare. Fares change, so check the price when you book.

**Journey time.** This guide does not give a travel time, because we could not find an official Nagoya–Gero figure on JR Central's site. Check the current timetable on [JR Central's train information pages](https://traininfo.jr-central.co.jp/zairaisen/index.html?lang=en) or at the station before you set your plans. Service on the Takayama Line can change for seasonal timetables and disruptions. For example, JR Central announced [temporary Hida schedules for winter 2025–26](https://jr-central.co.jp/news/release/_pdf/000044746.pdf) after rolling-stock problems.

If you are combining Gero with Takayama or other parts of central Japan, compare rail passes with point-to-point tickets before you buy. From Gero Station, the town's public baths and foot baths are within walking distance. Walking times are listed in each section below.

## Public baths you can use without staying overnight

You do not need a ryokan booking to bathe in Gero. The town has public bathhouses (*kyōdō yokujō*) that charge a small fee. You usually bring or buy your own towel and soap.

**Shirasagi-no-yu (白鷺乃湯).** A public bathhouse in the Yunoshima area, about a 7-minute walk from Gero Station. Hours are 10:00–20:45 (last entry 20:00). It closes on Wednesdays, or on Friday when Wednesday is a public holiday. Admission is 470 yen for adults, 180 yen for elementary school children and 100 yen for small children ([tourism association page](https://www.gero-spa.com/spot/detail_3.html), as of October 2026).

**Sachi-no-yu (幸乃湯).** About a 4-minute walk from the station. It opens 12:00–21:00 on weekdays and 11:00–21:00 on weekends and holidays (last entry 20:30). It closes on Tuesdays and the fourth Monday of the month. Admission is 470 yen for adults, 180 yen for elementary school children and 100 yen for small children. Its family baths are currently all suspended ([tourism association page](https://www.gero-spa.com/spot/detail_5.html), as of October 2026).

**Kur Garden Rotenburo (クアガーデン露天風呂).** About a 10-minute walk from the station. It offers six types of baths, including an open-air bath beside the Hida River. Hours are 8:00–20:45 (last entry 20:00), and it closes on Thursdays, or the following day when Thursday is a holiday. Admission is 800 yen for adults, 400 yen for elementary school children and 200 yen for small children ([tourism association page](https://www.gero-spa.com/spot/detail_4.html), as of October 2026).

**A note on Funsen-chi.** Older guides describe the open-air pool on the Hida riverbank as a free outdoor bath. According to the [tourism association](https://www.gero-spa.com/spot/detail_2.html), bathing there has been prohibited since December 1, 2021. You can still look at it, but do not plan to get in.

## Free foot baths around town

Foot baths (*ashiyu*) are the easiest way to try Gero's water. You just roll up your trousers and sit. The [Gifu Prefecture tourism site](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/article/detail_9.html) counts nine foot baths around town (as of October 2026). Bring a small towel to dry your feet.

- **Venus no Ashiyu (ビーナスの足湯):** A round foot bath that seats 12, about a 7-minute walk from the station. It is free and open 10:00–20:45, and it closes on Wednesdays ([tourism association page](https://www.gero-spa.com/spot/detail_9.html), as of October 2026).
- **Sagi no Ashiyu (鷺の足湯):** A roofed hinoki-wood foot bath that seats five, on Yunomachi-dori across from Kaeru Shrine. It is about an 8-minute walk from the station. It is free and open 24 hours ([tourism association page](https://www.gero-spa.com/spot/detail_6.html), as of October 2026).

The [tourism association's spot list](https://www.gero-spa.com/spot/) has more foot baths, including Miyabi no Ashiyu, Yuamiya no Ashiyu, Ta-no-Kami no Ashiyu and Sarubobo Ōgon no Ashiyu.

## The Yumeguri Tegata bath pass

The **Yumeguri Tegata** (湯めぐり手形) is a pass for day-use bathing at member ryokan, so day-trippers can try several inns' baths without booking a room. According to the [tourism association](https://www.gero-spa.com/spot/detail_50.html) (as of October 2026):

- **Price:** 2,500 yen per pass (adults, junior high school age and up)
- **Uses:** 3 baths
- **Validity:** 6 months
- **Children:** ages 4 to 12 (to the end of elementary school) bathe using a pass bought by an accompanying adult, and ages 0 to 3 bathe free
- **Includes:** a towel and a drawstring bag
- **Where to buy:** member ryokan, convenience stores and souvenir shops

Bathing hours and closed days differ from inn to inn, and some inns set specific times for pass holders. Check with each ryokan before you walk over. Because the pass is valid for half a year, you can split your three uses between a day trip and a later visit.

## Onsen etiquette basics

If this is your first Japanese hot spring, these basics apply at public baths and ryokan alike:

- **Wash before you soak.** Use the seated shower stations to wash and rinse your whole body before you get into the bath.
- **No swimwear in the bath.** Bathing is done nude in gender-separated baths. Bring a small towel for modesty, but keep it out of the water. Many people fold it on their head.
- **Keep your hair out of the water.** Tie long hair up.
- **Keep the water clean.** Do not wash clothes, use soap or shampoo in the tub, or swim.
- **Keep your voice down.** Baths are places to relax.
- **Dry off before the changing room.** Wipe yourself down with your small towel before you walk back in.
- **Tattoos.** Policies differ between facilities, so ask in advance.
- **Drink water.** Hot water dehydrates you, so rehydrate between baths.

If you would rather not bathe in front of others, see the notes on private baths below.

## Day trip or overnight?

**A day trip** suits you if your base is Nagoya and you mainly want to try the water. A realistic plan is one public bath or a few Yumeguri Tegata baths, a few foot baths, and a walk through town. You could also visit **Gero Onsen Gassho Village** (下呂温泉 合掌村), an open-air museum of ten thatched *gassho-zukuri* farmhouses. Some of them, including the Kyu Ōto residence, were moved from Shirakawa-go. It is about a 20-minute walk from the station and opens 8:30–17:00 (9:00–16:00 on Dec 31, Jan 1 and Jan 2), every day of the year. Admission is 800 yen for adults and 400 yen for elementary and junior high school students ([tourism association page](https://www.gero-spa.com/spot/detail_12.html), as of October 2026).

**Staying overnight** gives you the full ryokan experience: an evening bath, dinner, a quiet morning soak and a slower pace. It also fits well if you are continuing to Takayama the next day. In winter, the Gifu tourism site says the "Gero Onsen Hanabi Monogatari" fireworks are typically held on Saturdays from January to March ([kankou-gifu.jp](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/article/detail_9.html), as of October 2026). Confirm this year's dates on the [tourism association's event page](https://www.gero-spa.com/event/).

## How to choose a ryokan in Gero

We don't rank individual inns. These questions will help you narrow down the options on the [tourism association's accommodation listings](https://www.gero-spa.com/shukuhaku/) or a booking site.

**With or without meals?** A traditional stay includes a multi-course dinner and breakfast (*ippaku nishoku*), which is part of the experience but raises the price. Room-only or breakfast-only plans give you more flexibility and let you eat in town. Check dinner times, because many ryokan serve dinner early and at a set hour.

**Private baths.** If you are shy about communal bathing, have tattoos, or are traveling as a couple or family, look for one of these:
- a room with its own open-air bath (*rotenburo-tsuki kyakushitsu*)
- a reservable private bath (*kashikiri-buro*), which is often booked by time slot at check-in or in advance

**Near the station or by the river?** Inns near Gero Station are convenient if you arrive late, leave early or carry heavy luggage. Inns along the Hida River often have river or mountain views but may be a longer walk. Ask whether the inn offers a station pickup.

**Room style.** Japanese-style rooms have tatami mats and futon bedding laid out in the evening. Some inns also offer Western-style beds or combination rooms, which some travelers find more comfortable.

**Practical checks.** Look at check-in and check-out times, the bath hours (some baths switch between men and women overnight), the tattoo policy, dietary accommodations, and whether staff can communicate in English. If you are on a day trip, ask whether the inn accepts day-use bathers or the Yumeguri Tegata.

## FAQ

### How much is the train from Nagoya to Gero Onsen?
JR Central's [July 2025 press release](https://jr-central.co.jp/news/release/_pdf/000044393.pdf) lists the ticketless limited express charge for Nagoya to Gero on the Hida as 2,390 yen for an adult ordinary reserved seat and 4,660 yen for the Green Car, one way in the normal season (as of July 16, 2025). The base fare is extra.

### Can you bathe in Gero Onsen without staying at a ryokan?
Yes. Public bathhouses such as Shirasagi-no-yu, about a 7-minute walk from Gero Station, charge 470 yen for adults ([tourism association page](https://www.gero-spa.com/spot/detail_3.html), as of October 2026). Kur Garden Rotenburo, about a 10-minute walk from the station, charges 800 yen for adults ([tourism association page](https://www.gero-spa.com/spot/detail_4.html), as of October 2026).

### How does the Gero Onsen Yumeguri Tegata pass work?
The Yumeguri Tegata costs 2,500 yen per adult pass and covers 3 day-use baths at member ryokan, valid for 6 months, with a towel and a drawstring bag included ([tourism association](https://www.gero-spa.com/spot/detail_50.html), as of October 2026). It is sold at member ryokan, convenience stores and souvenir shops, and bathing hours differ from inn to inn.

### Can you still bathe at Funsen-chi in Gero?
No. According to the [tourism association](https://www.gero-spa.com/spot/detail_2.html), bathing at the open-air pool on the Hida riverbank has been prohibited since December 1, 2021. Visitors can still look at it.

### Is Gero Onsen better as a day trip or an overnight stay?
A day trip suits travellers based in Nagoya who mainly want to try the water at a public bath, a few foot baths or Yumeguri Tegata baths. Staying overnight gives the full ryokan experience with dinner and a morning soak, and fits well when continuing to Takayama the next day.
