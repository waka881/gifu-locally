Takayama's morning markets (asaichi) are one of the town's best-known sights, and one of the easiest to miss: they close at noon. This guide covers both markets, using information from Hida Takayama Tourism, the city's official tourism site.

## Takayama morning markets at a glance

| | Miyagawa Morning Market | Jinya-mae Morning Market |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Along the Miyagawa River, about 350 m between Kaji-bashi and Yayoi-bashi bridges | In front of Takayama Jinya |
| Size | About 60 shops and stalls (fewer in rain or winter) | Only farmers may open stalls |
| What's sold | Vegetables, fruit, pickles, spices; sweets and crafts such as sarubobo dolls and Ichii Itto-bori carvings | Fresh vegetables, dried foods, homemade pickles, wild vegetables |
| Hours (April–November) | 7:00–12:00 | 7:00–12:00 |
| Hours (December–March) | 8:00–12:00 | 8:00–12:00 |
| Entry | Free | Free |
| From JR Takayama Station | About 15 min on foot | About 10 min on foot (to Takayama Jinya) |

*Sources: [Hida Takayama Tourism](https://www.hida.jp/english/touristattractions/takayamacity/historyandculture/4000162.html); walking time to Takayama Jinya from [Hida Takayama Tourism](https://www.hida.jp/english/touristattractions/takayamacity/historyandculture/4000153.html), as of October 2026.*

## What time do the Takayama morning markets open?

From **7:00 to 12:00 from April to November**, and **8:00 to 12:00 from December to March**. Hida Takayama Tourism lists both markets as open all year round and suggests arriving around 6:00 to watch the vendors set up ([Hida Takayama Tourism](https://www.hida.jp/english/touristattractions/takayamacity/historyandculture/4000162.html), as of October 2026). It also notes the number of stalls can drop to about 10 in rain or in winter, so go on a dry morning if you can.

## Miyagawa or Jinya-mae: which market should you visit?

**Miyagawa** is the bigger one, with about 60 stalls on a 350 m stretch of the river. One side sells vegetables, fruit, pickles and spices; the other sells Japanese sweets and crafts, including chopsticks, sarubobo dolls and Ichii Itto-bori wood carvings (same source).

**Jinya-mae** is run by local growers: only farmers may open stalls, and it is known for unusual ingredients such as potato stems, carrot leaves and wild vegetables. Hida Takayama Tourism says it began over 300 years ago, when silk-raising farmers sold mulberry leaves (same source).

They are close enough to see both in one morning: start at Jinya-mae, then walk to the river. Takayama Jinya itself opens at 8:45 (¥500 for adults) if you want to look inside afterwards ([Takayama Jinya](https://jinya.gifu.jp/en/guide/info/), as of October 2026).

## Can you see the morning market on a day trip from Nagoya?

Not easily. The Limited Express Hida takes about 2 hours 20 minutes from Nagoya ([JR Central](https://global.jr-central.co.jp/en/goldenroute/area/hida-takayama/)), and the markets close at noon. The simple answer is to stay one night: arrive in the afternoon, see Sanmachi and Takayama Jinya, and do the markets the next morning before a bus to Shirakawa-go. ## Visiting in winter

The markets open an hour later, at 8:00, from December to March, and fewer stalls set up in snow or cold (same source). Combine an early market with a warm indoor sight, and see [Takayama in winter](takayama-in-winter.html) for snow and travel tips.

## FAQ

### What time does the Takayama morning market start?

7:00 from April to November and 8:00 from December to March; both markets close at 12:00 ([Hida Takayama Tourism](https://www.hida.jp/english/touristattractions/takayamacity/historyandculture/4000162.html), as of October 2026).

### Is the Takayama morning market open every day?

Hida Takayama Tourism lists both the Miyagawa and Jinya-mae markets as open all year round (same source). The number of stalls drops in rain and winter.

### How far is the Miyagawa morning market from Takayama Station?

About 15 minutes on foot from JR Takayama Station (same source).

### Which Takayama morning market is better?

Miyagawa has about 60 stalls including crafts and sweets; at Jinya-mae only farmers may open stalls, selling fresh and unusual local produce. They are close enough to visit both in one morning.
