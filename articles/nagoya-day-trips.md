Nagoya is a good base for exploring central Japan, and Gifu Prefecture starts just north of the city. Castle towns, blade-making workshops, paper-making streets, hot springs and thatched-roof villages are all close enough for a day trip, but how much time and planning each one takes varies a lot.

This guide ranks Gifu day trips from Nagoya by effort, from "hop on the next train" to "book your bus and leave early". Every travel time below comes from an official tourism or transport source, with the date we checked it. One note before you start: **Inuyama**, often listed among day trips from Nagoya, is in Aichi Prefecture, so it isn't covered here.

## 1. Gifu City: the easiest trip

**Getting there:** About 20 minutes by train between JR Nagoya Station and JR Gifu Station ([Visit Gifu](https://visitgifu.com/how-to-get-to/nagoya), as of October 2026).

**Who it suits:** First-time visitors, anyone with half a day, and travelers who like history.

**Highlight:** Ride the Mt. Kinka Ropeway up the mountain where Oda Nobunaga kept his castle (the keep itself is closed for renovation until late 2027, per the [Gifu CVB](https://www.gifucvb.or.jp/sightseeing/gifu-castle.php)), then walk the latticed townhouses of the Kawaramachi old merchant district ([Gifu Convention & Visitors Bureau](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/spot/910/)).

Gifu City is so close that you can start the morning in Nagoya and still sit down to breakfast in a Gifu coffee shop. Order a drink before late morning and toast or eggs often come with it. Read our guide to [Gifu's morning service](gifu-morning-service.html), then follow our [one-day Gifu City plan](gifu-city-one-day.html) for ropeway times and the old town.

## 2. Ogaki: castle town on the water

**Getting there:** Take the JR Tokaido Line from Nagoya to Ogaki Station ([Gifu tourism, Japanese](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/article/detail_282.html)). It's on the same line as Gifu City, a little further west. We couldn't find a travel time published by an official source, so check the JR Central timetable before you go.

**Who it suits:** Travelers who already know Gifu City and want a quieter town nearby, and anyone interested in the Battle of Sekigahara era.

**Highlight:** Ogaki Castle has a four-story keep, which the prefecture's tourism site describes as rare in Japan, and the Suimon River walk runs for 2.2 km past haiku monuments ([Gifu tourism, Japanese](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/article/detail_282.html), as of October 2026).

In spring, the city runs *tarai-bune* tub-boat rides along its waterways, about a 7-minute walk from JR Ogaki Station ([Visit Gifu](https://visitgifu.com/see-do/tarai-boat-river-cruise/)). Check the current season's dates before you plan around them.

## 3. Seki: knives and sword forging

**Getting there:** Take the train to JR Gifu Station (about 20 minutes, see above). From bus zone 14, take Gifu Bus B81 (Gifu Seki Line) to the Oyana stop: about 35–40 minutes ([Gifu Bus timetable](https://www.gifubus.co.jp/2610_gifu-seki.pdf), as of October 2026).

**Who it suits:** Cooks, knife collectors, and anyone who wants to watch traditional craftspeople at work.

**Highlight:** Seki has made blades for more than 700 years ([Go Central Japan](https://shoryudo.go-centraljapan.jp/en/sekiknifemuseum/)), and swordsmiths give public forging demonstrations on set days.

The transfer at Gifu adds a little planning, but it's still an easy half-day or full-day trip. The Seki Kaji Denshokan (Seki Swordsmith Museum) is closed for renovation until March 31, 2027, but its forging workshop stays open ([Gifu tourism, Japanese](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/spot/1179/), as of October 2026). Our [Seki knife guide](seki-knife-guide.html) has demonstration dates, a knife shop with an on-site forge, and tips for flying home with a blade.

## 4. Mino: washi paper and udatsu walls

**Getting there:** A highway bus on the Nagoya–Seki–Mino route runs from the Meitetsu Bus Center to the Chuno Sogo Chosha stop in Mino City. It takes 1 hour 30 minutes to 1 hour 50 minutes ([Gifu tourism, Japanese](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/article/detail_310.html), as of October 2026). The site doesn't list a fare, so check with the bus company.

**Who it suits:** Craft lovers, photographers, and travelers who prefer small towns to big sights.

**Highlight:** The Udatsu Townscape is a preserved merchant street designated a national Important Preservation District for Groups of Traditional Buildings. It's named for the *udatsu*, raised firewalls built at the ends of the roofs to stop fire spreading between houses ([Gifu tourism, Japanese](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/spot/1189/)).

Mino is known for **Mino washi**, handmade Japanese paper. At the Mino Washi Village Museum you can see how the paper is made and try making some yourself. In autumn, paper lantern artworks light up the old streets during the Mino Washi Lantern Art Exhibition ([Gifu tourism, Japanese](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/article/detail_310.html)). If you're coming by train from Gifu instead, the townscape is about a 10-minute walk from Mino-shi Station on the Nagaragawa Railway ([source](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/spot/1189/)).

## 5. Gujo Hachiman: castle town of clear water

**Getting there:** Highway buses run from Nagoya to Gujo Hachiman (as of April 1, 2026, per [Gifu tourism](https://www.kankou-gifu.jp/article/detail_370.html)):

- **JR Nagoya Station (Shinkansen side) to Gujo Hachiman IC:** about 1 hour 20 minutes, 6 buses each way, ¥2,600 adult one way (¥2,300 if you book online)
- **Meitetsu Bus Center to Gujo Hachiman IC:** about 1 hour 7 minutes, 3 buses each way, ¥2,400
- **Meitetsu Bus Center to Castle Town Plaza (in town):** about 1 hour 35 minutes, 1 bus each way, ¥2,400

**Who it suits:** Travelers who want a full day in a walkable old town, and anyone visiting during the summer dance festival.

**Highlight:** A wooden castle above a town of canals and matching wooden townhouses.

Gujo takes more effort because of the last leg. Most buses stop at the Gujo Hachiman IC bus stop on the expressway, not in town. The shared taxi into the center needs a reservation, so book it before you leave Nagoya. With only a few buses a day, also check the return times. Our [Gujo Hachiman day trip guide](gujo-hachiman-day-trip.html) covers the taxi, castle hours and autumn leaf dates.

## 6. Gero Onsen: a hot spring day out

**Getting there:** About 1 hour 40 minutes by JR Limited Express Hida between Nagoya Station and Gero Station ([Visit Gifu](https://visitgifu.com/how-to-get-to/nagoya), as of October 2026).

**Who it suits:** Anyone who wants a relaxing day with a hot spring bath, and travelers heading on to Takayama.

**Highlight:** Gero's soft, alkaline water is known for leaving skin feeling smooth. Around town you'll find free foot baths and the open-air Funsenchi pool ([JR Central](https://global.jr-central.co.jp/en/goldenroute/itineraries/takayama-gero-craftsmanship-onsen/)).

The train ride is longer, but it's a single direct limited express with no transfers or road traffic, which keeps the effort manageable. Many visitors stay the night, but a day trip works if you take an early train. See our [Gero Onsen guide](gero-onsen-guide.html) for bathing tips and what to see, and [Nagoya to Gero Onsen](nagoya-to-gero-onsen.html) for tickets and advance discounts.

## 7. Shirakawa-go: the most effort

**Getting there:** Two options, according to the Shirakawa-go Tourist Association ([official access page](https://shirakawa-go.gr.jp/en/access/), as of October 2026):

- **Express bus:** Walk about 5 minutes from Nagoya Station to the Meitetsu Bus Center, then take the express bus to Shirakawago Bus Terminal. The bus takes about 2 hours 30 minutes, or about 2 hours 50 minutes in total.
- **Train and bus:** Take the JR Limited Express Hida from Nagoya to Takayama (about 2 hours 20 minutes), then an express bus to Shirakawa-go (about 50 minutes). That's about 3 hours 25 minutes in total.

The official access page doesn't list fares. It links to bus reservation sites, so plan and book ahead.

**Who it suits:** Travelers who want to see the gassho-style farmhouses and don't mind a long day, or who can stay overnight.

**Highlight:** A village of steep thatched-roof houses, registered as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1995. More than 100 gassho-style buildings remain, and people still live in them ([Shirakawa-go Tourist Association](https://shirakawa-go.gr.jp/en/)).

Travel there and back takes up most of the day, so leave early and know your return bus time. Remember that you're visiting a place where people live. ## Tips for day trips from Nagoya

- **IC cards:** JR Central's TOICA card, and compatible cards, work within JR Central's IC card area. TOICA can't be used outside that area, except at specially marked stations, and generally can't be used for a trip that crosses from one IC card area into another. Check the area map before you head out to Gero or other towns further away ([JR Central](https://global.jr-central.co.jp/en/tickets/buy/toica/)).
- **Rail passes:** Several regional passes cover parts of Gifu, including the Japan Rail Pass and area passes such as the Takayama-Hokuriku Area Tourist Pass ([Visit Gifu](https://visitgifu.com/travel-to-gifu/)). Whether one is worth it depends on your route, so compare the price with individual tickets for the trips you actually plan. A pass usually makes more sense for the longer trips (Gero, Shirakawa-go) than for Gifu City.
- **Know which Nagoya terminal you need.** JR trains leave from Nagoya Station. Several highway buses leave from the Meitetsu Bus Center, about a 5-minute walk away ([Shirakawa-go Tourist Association](https://shirakawa-go.gr.jp/en/access/)). Some Gujo buses leave from the JR Nagoya Station (Shinkansen side) bus stop instead.
- **Check return times first.** On bus routes with only a few departures a day, the last bus home shapes your whole day.
- **Leave big bags in Nagoya.** Highway buses have limited luggage space. If you are between hotels, [Radical Storage lists luggage storage spots in Nagoya](https://radicalstorage.com/luggage-storage/nagoya), priced per bag per day (as of October 2026).
- **Combine nearby stops.** Gifu City, Seki and Mino are close to one another, so two of them can fit into one day.

Not sure where to start? For a first visit with little planning, Gifu City is the simplest choice. For a full day out, Gujo Hachiman or Gero Onsen. ## FAQ

### What is a good first day trip from Nagoya to Gifu?
Gifu City suits a first visit with little planning: it is about 20 minutes by train between JR Nagoya Station and JR Gifu Station ([Visit Gifu](https://visitgifu.com/how-to-get-to/nagoya), as of October 2026). It suits first-time visitors, anyone with half a day, and travelers who like history.

### How long does it take to get from Nagoya to Gero Onsen?
About 1 hour 40 minutes by JR Limited Express Hida between Nagoya Station and Gero Station ([Visit Gifu](https://visitgifu.com/how-to-get-to/nagoya), as of October 2026).

### How do you get from Nagoya to Shirakawa-go?
Walk about 5 minutes from Nagoya Station to the Meitetsu Bus Center and take the express bus to Shirakawago Bus Terminal, about 2 hours 50 minutes in total. Alternatively, take the JR Limited Express Hida to Takayama and an express bus from there, about 3 hours 25 minutes in total ([official access page](https://shirakawa-go.gr.jp/en/access/), as of October 2026).

### How do you get from Nagoya to Seki?
Take the train to JR Gifu Station (about 20 minutes), then Gifu Bus B81 (Gifu Seki Line) from bus zone 14 to the Oyana stop, about 35–40 minutes ([Gifu Bus timetable](https://www.gifubus.co.jp/2610_gifu-seki.pdf), as of October 2026).

### Is Inuyama in Gifu Prefecture?
No. Inuyama, often listed among day trips from Nagoya, is in Aichi Prefecture.
