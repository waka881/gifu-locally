Snow on the steep thatched roofs is the picture most people have of Shirakawa-go, and winter is when the village looks most like it. It is also when travel needs the most care. This guide covers when the snow comes, how to get there, what to wear and what changes in winter, using information from Shirakawa Village, the Shirakawa-go Tourist Association and the bus operators.

## When does Shirakawa-go get snow?

The Tourist Association says that in recent years the first snow falls around early December, and that snow keeps building from late December to late February ([Shirakawa-go Tourist Association FAQ](https://shirakawa-go.gr.jp/en/faq/4/), as of October 2026). February snow can reach 170 cm or more, and the record is 4.5 m ([Shirakawa-go Tourist Association](https://shirakawa-go.gr.jp/en/highlights/)). Shirakawa Village's own FAQ says snow falls every year from December to March ([Shirakawa Village](https://www.vill.shirakawa.lg.jp/1466.htm)).

**For the deepest snow, aim for late January to February.** Early December can be too early for a full snow cover, and by March the snow is melting.

All three need reserved seats on most services. In winter, Nohi Bus warns that delays of two hours or more can happen because of traffic waiting to park at Shirakawa-go, heavy snow or expressway closures ([Nohi Bus](https://www.nouhibus.co.jp/route_bus/shirakawago_en/)). Gifu Bus says it does not refund fares or cover missed connections caused by delays ([Gifu Bus](https://www.gifubus.co.jp/highway/shirakawago.html)). Don't book a tight train or flight on the same day.

## Can you drive to Shirakawa-go in winter?

You can, but Shirakawa Village says studless winter tyres are required, and recommends carrying tyre chains and using a 4WD vehicle ([Shirakawa Village FAQ](https://www.vill.shirakawa.lg.jp/1466.htm)). The Tourist Association also asks drivers to use studless tyres because the road surface is very slippery, and highly recommends 4WD ([Shirakawa-go Tourist Association FAQ](https://shirakawa-go.gr.jp/en/faq/4/)).

The village car park at Seseragi Park charges ¥2,000 for a standard car, 8:00–17:00 (last entry 16:30). On light-up days every vehicle needs a parking reservation ([Shirakawa-go Tourist Association](https://shirakawa-go.gr.jp/en/access/), as of October 2026). From December 1, 2026, the village is also introducing advance reservations for tour buses at its Seseragi Park Oro parking lot ([Shirakawa Village](https://www.vill.shirakawa.lg.jp/2897.htm)).

If you are not used to snow driving, a bus or a guided tour is the simpler choice.

## What should you wear?

Shirakawa Village advises warm clothing, gloves and winter footwear, and says temperatures can stay below freezing even in the daytime. It warns that slips are common even when it is not snowing, and suggests rainwear or a poncho rather than an umbrella when snow is falling ([Shirakawa Village FAQ](https://www.vill.shirakawa.lg.jp/1466.htm)). Its winter page recommends long boots or footwear with deep treads because icy roads are slippery ([Shirakawa Village](https://www.vill.shirakawa.lg.jp/1521.htm)).

- Waterproof boots with deep treads (or add-on grips)
- Warm layers, a hat and gloves
- A rain jacket or poncho for falling snow
- A bag for your trash: there are no bins in the village ([Shirakawa-go Tourist Association FAQ](https://shirakawa-go.gr.jp/en/faq/5/))

## What changes in winter?

- **Shorter hours at some sights.** The Gassho-zukuri Minkaen open-air museum opens 9:00–16:00 from December to February and closes on Thursdays from December to March ([Shirakawa-go Tourist Association](https://shirakawa-go.gr.jp/en/active/7/)).
- **Early dark.** Day visitors are not admitted to the village after dark, to protect residents' lives and privacy; only overnight guests can be there at night, except on light-up evenings ([Shirakawa-go Tourist Association](https://shirakawa-go.gr.jp/en/events/106/)).
- **Coin lockers** at the bus terminal are available 8:00–17:00 ([Shirakawa-go Tourist Association FAQ](https://shirakawa-go.gr.jp/en/faq/5/)).

## When is the Shirakawa-go light-up?

The 41st Shirakawa-go Light-Up is on **January 11, 17, 24 and 31, 2027** ([Shirakawa-go Tourist Association](https://shirakawa-go.gr.jp/), as of October 2026). The 2026 event was reservation-only, and lodging handled by the Tourist Association is allocated by lottery. Our [light-up guide](shirakawa-go-light-up.html) explains how to attend.

## Should you stay overnight?

Staying in a farmhouse inn is the only way to see the village at night outside the light-up dates, and it avoids racing the last bus in snow; see [staying overnight in Shirakawa-go](shirakawa-go-overnight-stay.html). The Tourist Association's accommodation search lists 41 places to stay ([Shirakawa-go Tourist Association](https://shirakawa-go.gr.jp/en/stay/), as of October 2026). Most travellers base themselves in Takayama instead, about 50 minutes away by bus; see [where to stay in Takayama](where-to-stay-takayama.html) and [Takayama in winter](takayama-in-winter.html).

## FAQ

### Is Shirakawa-go worth visiting in winter?

Yes, if you want the classic view of snow on the thatched roofs: snow builds up from late December to late February, and February snow can reach 170 cm or more ([Shirakawa-go Tourist Association](https://shirakawa-go.gr.jp/en/faq/4/), as of October 2026). Plan for bus delays and cold.

### When is the best time to see snow in Shirakawa-go?

Late January to February, when the snow is deepest. The first snow usually falls around early December, and snow keeps accumulating until late February (same source).

### Do I need snow tyres to drive to Shirakawa-go?

Yes. Shirakawa Village says studless tyres are required in winter and recommends chains and 4WD ([Shirakawa Village FAQ](https://www.vill.shirakawa.lg.jp/1466.htm)).

### Can I see Shirakawa-go at night in winter?

Only on the light-up evenings (January 11, 17, 24 and 31, 2027) or if you stay overnight in the village. Day visitors are not admitted after dark on other nights ([Shirakawa-go Tourist Association](https://shirakawa-go.gr.jp/en/events/106/)).
